`DynString`, `Bytes` and `DynArray` own their allocations and free them when dropped, so they no longer leak on native targets. They are no longer `Copy`: use `clone()` for a second value, or a view parameter to read one without moving it. `from_abi_tail` now takes ownership of the allocation it is given, so a pointer into another owner, such as a `MemBuffer`, must be copied first. `DynString::as_bytes`, whose result outlived the string, is replaced by `into_bytes`, which takes the string and keeps its allocation. `PackedUserOperation` is no longer `Copy`, and functions that send a `Bytes` or `DynArray` in a message, such as the ERC-721 and ERC-1155 helpers, take it by `own`. `DynArray::from_slice` takes `T: Clone`, and `dyn_array_from` no longer needs `T: Copy`. Fixed arrays encode and decode without `T: Copy`, so `#[abi]`, `#[event]` and `#[error]` structs can hold fixed arrays of non-`Copy` elements.
