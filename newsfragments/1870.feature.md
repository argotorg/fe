Owned values are destroyed exactly once when their owner gives them up: at the end of its scope, when overwritten, or for the fields a partial move leaves behind. A type releases what it holds in a `core::ops::Drop` implementation; `core::mem::drop` destroys a value early and `core::mem::forget` gives it up without destroying it. `MemVec`, `MemArray`, `MemBuffer` and `FixedMemBuffer` free their allocation and destroy their elements, and the new `core::ptr::Box<T>` holds one value in an allocation of its own. Operations that allocate gain fallible forms, such as `try_with_capacity`, `try_reserve`, `MemVec::try_push` and `core::ptr::try_alloc_bytes`, and `core::ptr::dealloc` frees raw allocations on native targets. A type may hold itself through a generic type that keeps its argument behind a pointer, as in `struct Tree { children: MemVec<Tree> }`. See `docs/language/destruction.md`.
