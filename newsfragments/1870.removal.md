Memory owners now destroy what they hold, which changes the meaning of some existing code. `v[i] = x` and `set` destroy the element they overwrite, so fill the uninitialized elements of `MemArray::new_uninit` or `core::ptr::alloc_array` through raw places (`*array.ptr_at(i) = x`). On native targets, a pointer into an owner dangles once the owner is destroyed: keep the owner alive, or `core::mem::forget` it. `MemArray::into_bytes` and `into_buffer` require `Copy` elements. `std::native::ByteBuffer` and `FixedStack` are removed in favor of `MemBuffer` and `MemVec`, which need no `release` call: indexing replaces `byte_at` and `set_byte`, and `copy_within` and `copy_from` are on `buffer.span()` and its slices. Native `extern` declarations of `malloc` and `free` are rejected in favor of `core::ptr::try_alloc_bytes` and `core::ptr::dealloc`.
